How this model works & source references

What the green intervals mean

Observation is calculated from the actual route, aircraft attitude, sensor pointing envelope, field of view, and the assumed slant-range cutoff. The sensor uses ideal pointing within its envelope. Terrain, cloud, atmospheric quality, acquisition delay, and tracking-rate limits are not modeled.

These four editable profiles are examples, not specifications for named operational aircraft. Their pattern menus are demonstration choices. A field of view is an instantaneous footprint; a pointing envelope is the region a sensor can aim into.

For a repeating route, an observation spanning the start/end seam is merged. Open area searches stop after one pass. A completely visible route is labeled continuous within the modeled duration.

Reading the display

The amber point is the object being observed. Move it to compare an orbit around a point with an offset observation geometry. Survey mode points the sensor down; its footprint moves with the aircraft. The metrics describe this one reference point, not complete coverage of the surrounding area.

Bank checks are a simplified coordinated-turn feasibility check. When a route exceeds the profile's assumed bank limit, the displayed access describes the geometric route only.
